Behind the Project

This section presents the step-by-step workflow used to build and structure the project.

  • Step 1 – Selection

    Everything began with identifying the research question, the fundamental cornerstone upon which the entire project is built.
    From the very beginning, it was clear that the focus had to be the renowned historical novel I promessi sposi, written by Alessandro Manzoni and published in 1840, the final edition.
    What was not immediately clear, however, was which specific aspect of the novel to explore.

    After careful analysis and several initial attempts, the choice ultimately fell on investigating the powerful and meaningful connection between the historical events and the plot of the novel.
    The work itself is exceptionally long, consisting of 38 chapters, highly complex, and incredibly rich in content. It deals with profound and intense themes that invite deep personal reflection, such as Divine Providence, which guides and saves the humble; the contrast between power and weakness; the oppression of the powerful over the weak; as well as justice and revenge, the evolution from reaction to righteous action, and ultimately, forgiveness.
    Moreover, the novel explores the suffering of the innocent, faith and doubt, the human condition (love, pain, hope), and a critique of power and authority. The importance of these themes is undeniable both on social and personal level.
    Indeed, there already exist countless analyses, studies, websites, and digital projects dedicated to Manzoni’s masterpiece.

    However, one aspect that is often overlooked — almost taken for granted — is the strong and intricate relationship between history and plot that runs throughout the entire novel.
    This project seeks to shed light on a further additional connection, particularly by distinguishing between real historical events mentioned in the narrative and fictional episodes created by the author. It is precisely this blend of history and imagination that defines I promessi sposi as a historical novel.

    The ultimate goal of this project is to analyze the relationship between history and plot and the reality–fiction dichotomy, visually representing both through a vertical timeline , which serves as the beating heart of the website titled Manzoni Storytelling.

    The identified objectives must also be adapted to the type of target audience to which the project is addressed.
    In order to select the right target audience, a careful reasoning process was carried out, taking into account the skills and background of the creators.
    Considering that only a figure extremely specialized in the subject of Manzoni and his works would be able to meet the needs and respond to the requests that might arise from other experts in the field, it became clear that the project was not sufficiently in-depth and detailed for an expert audience, who could have criticized it for not being specific enough.
    Instead, it proved to be much more suitable for an audience of students who have already heard about Manzoni and his works during previous years of schooling but who still do not have full mastery of the topics.
    For this reason, a project with a didactic approach would be particularly appropriate for them.
    In light of this reasoning, it seemed evident to create the project as an educational support tool that helps students not only to learn information about one of the most famous authors, Manzoni, and his incredible literary works, but also to memorize the concepts through distinctive features of the project and interactive, engaging exercises.

  • Step 2 – Planification

    Once the topic was chosen and the specific aspect of analysis was defined, the research phase began.
    The starting point for the entire project was an important synoptic table comparing two important works of Manzoni: Fermo e Lucia (the very first draft of I Promessi Sposi, dating back to around 1821) and I Promessi Sposi (the final 1840 edition).
    The two novels were divided into numbered narrative sequences presented in ascending order, accompanied by short summaries related to the original texts.
    The table was created by Professor Paola Italia, in collaboration with philologists and other experts in the literary and linguistic field, all specialists in the study of Manzoni and his works.

    Fig. n. Synoptic table of the novels Fermo e Lucia and I promessi sposi divided in narrative sequences sequences.



    As you can see, the table is organized into four columns:
    • The first two are dedicated to Fermo e Lucia: the first column indicates the sequence number, while the second provides brief summaries.
    • The last two are dedicated to I Promessi Sposi, structured in the same way.

    This structure makes it possible to clearly see the process of writing and rewriting, as well as the additions, omissions, and changes that Manzoni developed over time.
    Analyzing both novels in detail would have been too time-consuming for this project, so the focus shifted exclusively to I Promessi Sposi and to the relationship between story and plot within the novel, using the main sequences identified in the synoptic table as reference points.
    Although the table was created at the end of the 20th century, it remains remarkably innovative and modern in terms of its structure and precision of content. However, its visual representation is not as modern or engaging, it appears as a simple, flat table, which is not very appealing in today’s digital context.
    For this reason, a new and more dynamic graphical representation was chosen, a timeline.
    To fully exploit the potential of digital technology, the table was uploaded into a Google artificial intelligence tool that functions as an assistant for document analysis and management named NotebookLM along with the I Promessi Sposi PDF to extract the most significant dates and related key events from the novel.

    Fig. n.1. PDF of I promessi sposi (Quarantana)



    Another key reference and essential source used after identifying the objectives, the target audience, and establishing the main structure, particularly for creating the content, was the textbook I promessi sposi, edited by Natalino Sapegno and Goizio Viti (consulted in PDF format for this project), as a model.
    This book proved to be extremely useful and well suited to the task, as it was specifically designed to help and guide students in understanding such a complex novel.



    Fig. n.2. PDF of I promessi sposi edited by Natalino Sapegno and Goizio Viti



    Once the list of meaningful dates and their corresponding events was obtained, the next step was to design and plan the timeline in Canva, in order to quickly and precisely identify the main features to present.
    Sveral attempts were made before reaching the final layout. Below you can see the first and the last drafts of models, but in between several other models were created, some of which included minor as well as significant modifications.


    Fig. n.3. Canva general strucure timeline and boxes layout sketches



    The timeline is vertically oriented and centrally positioned to ensure accessibility and readability across all digital devices. It includes connections on both the right and left sides, enhancing your engagement and understanding.
    As you can see in the first picture, the events displayed on the left side of the timeline, marked in blue, follow the chronological order of the story, while those on the right, marked in orange, follow the narrative order, that is, the sequence in which the author chose to present them, including flashbacks and flashforwards.
    As you can see in the second and in the third picture, once the main structure had been established, the next step was to organize each box in a clear and orderly way.
    The final choice, whether the boxes are arranged in chronological or narrative order, requires that each box includes a title summarizing the event that occurred on the given date and then by a preview of the summary of the sequence in English.
    In addition to this, by clicking the Read more button a popup opens, showing first the sequence summary in English and then the original reference text in Italian. The texts can be easily read using the sidebar, which allows you to scroll up and down.

  • Step 3 – Development

    Based on this representation, the actual digitalization of the idea began using languages such as HTML, CSS, JavaScript, and some supporting templates.

    First, it was necessary to create a digital environment to host the timeline. A website, easily understandable and usable, especially for high school students studying Alessandro Manzoni as an author and I promessi sposi as his work, proved to be the best choice.
    The website was built starting from a template provided by html5up.net, which served as a base and was completely customized in terms of layout, colors, fonts, and content.
    For the timeline, the Vertical Timeline model from CodePen.io was used and customized based on the project previously created in Canva, in order to create a project as close as possible to the original idea.


    As such, even in the digital version, events arranged in chronological order appear on the left side, while those following the narrative order are on the right.
    Two representative Flaticon icons were chosen for clarity:

    • Chronological order is represented by a yellow clock icon on a brown background.
    • Narrative order is represented by two intertwined arrows pointing in opposite directions, blue and orange on a yellow background.

    Fig. n.4. the watch icon represents the story order while the tangle icon represents the plot order in which events are arranged.



    The boxes were filled with the relevant dates, events, and summaries.

    As you browse through the boxes, you’ll notice a small Flaticon icon of an open book placed next to each introductory title. Inside the icon, a number appears on the two pages: this identifies the number of the chapter that the sequence refers to.
    When you hover over the icon, a small popup appears, clearly explaining the chapter reference and the purpose of the symbol.

    Including this icon was a deliberate choice. It offers you an immediate way to understand how each sequence is related to the broader structure of the novel—helping reveal the intricate relationship between story and plot, as well as the many flashbacks and flashforwards that shape the story’s timeline.

    Fig. n.5. The open-book icon indicates the number of the chapter.



    Additionally, it was decided to further analyze the link between historically accurate events integrated into the novel, which provide a historical background to the story, and purely fictional events invented by the author.
    To highlight this distinction, which is essentially why the novel is considered a cornerstone of historical fiction, two Flaticon icons were placed in the corners of the boxes:
    • For historically accurate events, a pair of gray swords in the bottom right corner of boxes on the left side of the timeline,
    • For purely fictional events, a magic wand in the bottom left corner of boxes on the right side of the timeline.

    Fig. n.6. the magic wand icon represents the fictional events while the swords icon represents the real historical events inserted in the novel.



    All these icons are presented and explained in an introductory legend that is accessible by clicking on the legend button displayed on the right part of the screen above the entire timeline.

    Fig. n.7. the legend button: once clicked, the button displays an explanation of the all the icons.


    Once the timeline page, the core of the entire project, was created, it was time to design and develop the other sections: six main sections form the core of the website:
    Home, The Project, Before You Begin, Time & Space, Try It Yourself, and Behind the Project.

    These sections are always visible in the top navigation bar, giving you an immediate overview as soon as they enter the site.
    Some of these sections expand further: one of them includes four subsections, while three others each contain three subsections.
    This organization was chosen to bring clarity and order to a website rich in content, helping you easily navigate the variety of materials available.
    Here there is a very brief explanation of the contents and special features you can find in all the pages:

    • First, the Homepage was created: the site’s first page, serving as an introductory showcase that you can see immediately upon opening the website. This page introduces the project and provides a general overview of the entire theme, also offering two direct links to the site’s key pages: the timeline and the project page.

      Fig. n.8. Home page preview.



    • Next, the Project page was developed: it provides a detailed overview of the entire work, outlining all the different sections that make up the website and explaining their content to help you navigate more easily, saving time and effort. This page also features an engaging carousel that gives a preview of all the pages.

      Fig. n.9. Project page preview.



    • Then, the Before You Begin section ws created. This is not a real page but a thematic container that groups the pages you can actually access. It gathers all the essential background knowledge that you should understand while reading the novel and before interacting with the project's main tools.
      These subsections introduce I promessi sposi and include:

      1. Author page provides bibliographical information about Alessandro Manzoni, the renowned author of the historical novel I Promessi Sposi. This allows you to explore his family, cultural, political, geographical, and social background, which significantly influenced the writing of his works.

        Fig. n.10. Author page preview.

      2. Literary work page explains the various stages of writing and rewriting that the novel went through before reaching its final form in the 1840 publication and also provides a summary of the novel. Through the sidebar on the right side of the screen, you can explore the main and most famous characters of the story, as well as the locations mentioned, each linked to their respective subpages for further exploration.

        Fig. n.11. Literary Work page preview.

      3. Characters page presents a brief introduction to the most well-known characters of the novel, accompanied by graphic representations taken from Gonin’s illustrations included in the final 1840 edition of the novel.

        Fig. n.12. Characters page preview.

      4. Places page offers a brief introduction to the most notable locations in the novel, also featuring graphic representations based on Gonin’s illustrations from the 1840 edition.

        Fig. n.13. Places page preview.



    • Subsequently, the Time & Space section was developed. Again, it is only a container that groups the project’s core analytical tools, which make the website unique among platforms dedicated to I promessi sposi.
      These tools are:

      1. Already analyzed earlier, but also part of the website’s sections, is the Timeline page: this page presents the complex connection between the fabula and the plot of the novel. The events on the left are arranged according to the story order (that is, the chronological order), while those on the right follow the narrative order, meaning the sequence in which the author chose to include them in the story. Each box corresponds to an event, and for each one it is possible to read both a summary of the event and the original text excerpt simply by clicking the Read more button, which opens a popup displaying both texts. Inside the boxes, there are also two icons that highlight the difference between fictional events purely invented by the author and historical events that truly occurred — a blend that gives the novel one of its most distinctive features, making it a classic example of a historical novel. The magic wand icon refers to fictional events, while the two crossed swords icon represents real historical facts.

        Fig. n.14. Timeline page preview.


        The timeline structure itself is the result of careful customization of the Vertical Timeline Template available on the Codepen website.
      2. The novel and Contemporary European Culture page links Manzoni’s personal life occurrences to the events that happen in Italy and in Europe at the same time.
        You have two types of timelines available, horizontal and vertical, to explore, deepen, and compare events.

        In the horizontal timeline, you can select a date by clicking directly on it, or use the two small circles at the ends of the timeline to move forward or backward in time and choose the event you want to explore. Once a date is selected, three categories appear: In Europe, In Italy, and Manzoni and the writing of the novel.


        Fig. n.15. The novel and Contemporary European Culture page: Horizontal Timeline preview.



        The vertical timeline follows the structure used to analyze the relationship between fabula and intreccio. The boxes along the timeline are listed in chronological order from 1821 to 1842, with each box showing the specific year it refers to.
        Boxes on the left side correspond to events in Manzoni’s private life and his career as a writer.
        Boxes on the right side display the main events taking place in Italy and Europe during the same year.

        Fig. n.16. The novel and Contemporary European Culture page: Vertical Timeline preview.



        The information used to create the content for both timelines is drawn from the textbook I promessi sposi, edited by Natalino Sapegno and Goizio Viti.

      3. Map page focuses on Northern Italy—especially Lombardy, the main setting of I Promessi Sposi.
        It visualizes the novel’s locations, highlights which setting dominates each of the first eleven chapters, and tracks the movements of the protagonists, Renzo and Lucia. The map has three menus that allows you to visualize:
        1. Places: identifies and lists all the main locations in which the novel takes place,
        2. Chapters: shows and highlights which setting dominates each of the first eleven chapters,
        3. Characters:tracks the movements of the protagonists, indicating whether Renzo, Lucia, or both are present in a location.
        A Legend button provides additional details to help distinguish the three different kinds of marker which were carefully created with Storepoint and Removebg and associated to each menu.

      1. Narrative Sequences Challenge page presents the first engaging exercise whose goal is to ask you to reconstruct the plot of the first 7 chapters by dragging and dropping the narrative sequences into the correct order.
        Furthermore, by completing the exercise, you have the chance to win the The MANZONIAN LICENSE which tests whether you fully understand I Promessi Sposi, especially the order and interconnection of the main events.

      2. How well do you know the characters? page presents an interesting exercise which lets you test your knowledge of the characters of I Promessi Sposi.
        It helps students understand the novel’s complex characters.
        Each box represents a character and contains a multiple-choice quiz, only one correct answer per question.

      3. Which character are you? page shows an intimate exercise which allows you to reflect on your personality.

  • Step 4 – Website Creation

    As already suggested, the project was created as a digital tool specifically designed for secondary school students who need to deepen their study of a renowned author like Alessandro Manzoni and, in particular, his masterpiece, I Promessi Sposi.
    Manzoni Storytelling aims to serve as a supportive tool for students.

    This tool was originally developed as an internship thesis project for the Digital Humanities and Digital Knowledge program at the University of Bologna and was created in collaboration with, and with the valuable support of, the company Sutech in Salerno, which contributed its expertise and professionalism in the digital field.
    The basic framework of the site was built using HTML and CSS, while the main color palette was chosen based on LeggoManzoni, a well-known website dedicated to the author and his work I Promessi Sposi.

    To enrich the site and make it more engaging, JavaScript scripts and both animated and static icons from Flaticon were also used. This results in a site with a simple and clear layout that nonetheless offers many interesting and engaging stimuli and calls to action that consistently capture your attention.

    Additionally, to enhance professionalism, pre-existing Codepen templates were used and customized down to the smallest detail to meet the project’s requirements—particularly for the timelines (both the two vertical ones and the horizontal one) and for the multiple-choice quizzes and drag-and-drop exercises.

    Currently, the site is available at… [SITE PUBLICATION]

    The site is rich in content and additional links designed to improve learning and the retention of concepts related to Manzoni and his novel.
    The website already boasts a remarkable advantage that makes it incredibly cutting-edge and up-to-date, giving it excellent potential for use all around the world.
    The site can be accessed not only in English but also in many other languages, making it exceptionally accommodating for you as a user.
    This major benefit has been implemented using the existing code provided by W3Schools which allows for authomatic trnaslation of the texts.

    Despite this, there is still room for many improvements, both stylistic and textual:

    • For what concernes the content: It would be interesting to add the missing chapters of the novel, from 12 to 38 (the actual final chapter), with their corresponding representation on the timelines and map.
    • For what concernes the basic information: Presenting all characters and describing all locations in the novel would provide a richer and more complete general overview.
    • Fot what concernes the map visualization: It would be useful to display all locations where the novel’s scenes take place, as well as the movements of all characters throughout the story.
